abstract: Laser guidance systems are valued for their high precision, long engagement
  range, and resilience against electronic countermeasures, making them indispensable
  in modern precision strike operations. However, atmospheric disturbances, especially
  turbulence-induced wavefront distortions and aerosol-induced extinction attenuation,
  seriously degrade the optical sensing response and guidance-related measurement
  accuracy over long distances. Existing studies often examine turbulence or aerosol
  effects in isolation, without providing a unified framework that links these disturbances
  directly to seeker optical response metrics. This paper develops a high-fidelity,
  system-level simulation framework that models the complete process of laser emission,
  atmospheric propagation, target reflection, and seeker reception. The framework
  couples multilayer turbulence modeling with altitude-dependent profiles, aerosol
  extinction and scattering effects derived from MODTRAN data, and wavefront evolution
  using the angular spectrum method. By directly computing seeker optical response
  indicators such as angular error, centroid shift, and energy concentration, the
  model quantitatively evaluates how turbulence strength, aerosol concentration, laser
  power, and propagation distance interact to affect guidance precision. Validation
  against representative experimental data demonstrates good agreement and confirms
  the model’s capability to reproduce the statistical behavior of the measured seeker
  response. The results further clarify the distinct roles of turbulence-induced phase
  distortion and aerosol-induced amplitude attenuation, and provide quantitative operating
  envelopes for laser power and propagation distance. These findings establish both
  a theoretical foundation and a practical simulation tool to support the optimization
  of laser-guided weapon systems under complex atmospheric conditions.
